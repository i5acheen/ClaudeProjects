## 3. Services & treatments

### 3.1 Varicose veins (main specialty)
- **What they are:** Swollen, dilated, twisted, bulging veins in the legs. They start as blue or greyish lines and over time bulge and darken.
- **Common symptoms:** itching or burning, swelling and pain, tiredness and heaviness in the legs, muscle cramps, bulging veins, skin colour changes. Aching is often worse after activity or at night. Some patients get cramps on standing up suddenly, and many get restless legs.
- **Possible complications if ignored:** vein rupture and bleeding, night-time pain, leg cramps, swelling, deformity.
- **Why they happen:** damaged or weak one-way valves in the leg veins let blood pool. Causes include long hours of standing, valve damage and family history.
- **Diagnosis:** clinical examination plus Colour Doppler (USG Doppler) of the leg, which shows whether varicose veins are present and how far the disease has spread.
- **Treatment options (depends on the stage of disease):**
  - Counselling: simple preventive measures and lifestyle changes
  - Medical management: medicines
  - Day-care **Endovenous LASER treatment**
  - Sclerotherapy combined with ablation (for single perforator disease)
  - Newer options: **Cyanoacrylate glue (VenaSeal™)**, a non-thermal closure, and **Mechanochemical ablation (MOCA)**
- **Benefits of endovenous laser treatment (per website):**
  - Day-care procedure, no long hospital stay
  - Patients can usually walk home and return to work soon after
  - Only a small puncture that heals quickly, so the website calls it scarless and painless
- **Aftercare (per website):**
  - Discharge the same day or the next day
  - Oral medicines for the first 3–5 days, plus regular follow-ups
  - Avoid standing for long, vigorous activity, gym and running for 7–14 days
  - Wear compression stockings about 8–10 hours a day for 3–6 months
  - No bed rest needed; normal daily activities can resume
- **Recurrence:** Many patients need no further treatment after laser. Lifestyle changes and stockings keep the chance of recurrence low.
- **Prevention / self-care tips:**
  - Avoid sitting (especially cross-legged) or standing in one position for long
  - Raise the legs while resting
  - Exercise and lose weight gradually
  - Avoid tobacco
  - On long flights or long sitting, get up and walk every hour
  - Compression stockings can help; choose them on the doctor's advice

### 3.5 Deep Vein Thrombosis (DVT)
- **What it is:** blood clots in the deep veins of the arms or legs. Dangerous because a clot can travel to the lungs (pulmonary embolism), which can be life-threatening.
- **Risk factors:** age over 50, immobility, smoking, pregnancy or the period after delivery, birth control pills, family history, obesity.
- **Symptoms:** warmth and redness, leg swelling, cramps starting in the calf, unexplained severe pain in the foot or ankle.
- **Treatment:** usually blood thinners and compression stockings. Acute DVT (within 14 days) is best treated endovascularly: an IVC filter is placed, clots are removed through a catheter, and clot-dissolving medicine is given locally. This is done through a tiny hole with no cuts or stitches, and the patient can usually go home the next day.
- **Aftercare:** compression stockings, leg elevation, blood thinners for about 6 months, walking at least 1 hour a day.

### 3.6 Peripheral Vascular / Arterial Disease (PVD / PAD)
- Narrowing or blockage of leg arteries from plaque. Causes pain on walking that eases with rest (claudication). If untreated, it can lead to loss of toes, fingers or a limb.
- **Tests:** Doppler ultrasound, Ankle-Brachial Index (ABI), angiogram (CT/MR/DSA).
- **Treatment:** depends on the symptoms and Doppler/angiography results. Severe blockages may need angioplasty and stenting.
